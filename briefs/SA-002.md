# SA-002 — Formation fleet and sine-wave dives

Producer status: SPECIFIED. Revision to SA-001, previously QA READY. Project: D:/Downloads/TRABALHO/BLUESPOT DS/Space Attack G2i.

## Request and scope
Confirmed: preserve the graphics; enemies remain in formation at the top, individually descend along sine-wave dive paths while shooting toward the player; player moves only left/right at the bottom; each stage increases enemy movement speed. Preserve existing score, health, start, game over, restart and pause behavior.

Routine assumptions: stage equals the existing numbered wave. Enemies occupy spaced rows/columns in the upper playfield and wait in formation until their staggered turn to dive; several dives can overlap within a small fixed concurrency limit. Surviving divers loop back into their formation slot after crossing below the visible playfield, then become eligible for another dive. A stage clears only after every enemy is destroyed, either by player fire or ship contact. Returning divers award no score and do not count as destroyed. Enemy aimed fire targets the player's position at emission, then travels on a straight trajectory. No new assets, redesign, weapons, audio or touch support.

## Behavior and boundaries
1. Anchor player at the existing bottom baseline (h - 85 or equivalent visually safe lower margin). Support A/D and Left/Right; W/S/Up/Down must never change the player's Y. Update onscreen legend and README to accurately describe horizontal controls. Keep horizontal speed, Space cadence, P/Enter/button behaviors and damage/scoring rules.
2. Each stage spawns enemies at visibly separated formation slots in the top region below the HUD. Arrange the existing count cap (24) responsively, with no clipping or enemy HUD overlap at either target viewport. Formation may have a small synchronized lateral drift; it must not continuously descend.
3. Schedule staggered individual dives automatically with a documented cadence and bounded concurrency. Every surviving slot is eligible and must eventually receive a turn; do not indefinitely privilege one enemy or leave a non-diving fleet. Show a meaningful formation period before the first dive and gaps between departures.
4. A dive progresses downward continuously and uses a sine function for lateral displacement relative to its starting/home X. Make oscillation obvious across the descent, with at least one complete left/right excursion. Clamp or scale amplitude near edges so the enemy remains visible while within the playfield. Avoid identical synchronized bulk descent.
5. Divers fire aimed projectiles toward the player during descent, using current player position when each shot is created. Formation enemies may remain silent; shooting by a diving enemy is required. Skip firing after it exits below the playfield. Existing projectile cleanup, collision damage (20), immunity (1.1 seconds) and enemy destruction score (100) remain.
6. After crossing the lower boundary, a surviving diver returns to its original slot as a fresh formation occupant, without score, health loss solely for escape, or removal from the stage. A simple reset to home is acceptable; no visible travel back through the arena is necessary. Clear dive state/timer consistently and retain fairness in scheduling.
7. Increase enemy movement speed on each stage. Use a strictly increasing documented function for dive vertical speed and any formation drift that constitutes movement. The old hard speed cap must not flatten the new stage-speed criterion. Other difficulty parameters may retain their existing caps. Keep delta-time safety and bounded entity count; do not add bulk projectiles on returns.
8. Resize recomputes formation slots and bottom player baseline, keeps live divers/bullets safe, and cannot restart the wave or duplicate a loop. Restart removes all formation/dive scheduling state and begins stage 1 with full health and zero score. Pause freezes formation movement, dive progression and timers; resume clears stale held keys.
